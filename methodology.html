Methodology

From physical harm to financial liability.

ReVerity separates scientific measurement, financial assessment, legal authority, and restoration allocation. Each discipline is independently reviewable — and no layer can silently substitute for another.

Core Methodology Chain

Every liability traces an unbroken chain of evidence.

Environmental debt accounting begins with an identified actor and ends with a restoration need. Between them, planetary boundary accounting connects each measured harm to the public burden it creates — and to the financial liability that burden represents.

Actor→ Facility→ Activity→ Environmental Harm→ Boundary Impact→ Public Burden→ Financial Liability→ Restoration Need

A liability figure that cannot walk this chain, link by link, does not enter the ledger.

Three-Layer Methodology

Measurement, weighting, and rate are kept separate.

Environmental financial accountability fails when science, severity, and price collapse into one number. ReVerity holds the three layers apart so each can be challenged, corrected, and audited on its own terms.

Layer 01 — Science

Physical Measurement

What actually happened, in physical units.

  • Tons of CO₂e emitted
  • Pounds of PFAS released
  • Gallons of contaminated discharge
  • Tons of waste disposed
  • Acres of land degraded
  • Quantity of water extracted
  • Concentration above regulatory limits
Layer 02 — Severity

Environmental Debt Factor

How much harm each unit represents.

  • Toxicity and persistence
  • Mobility and exposure pathways
  • Affected population
  • Ecological sensitivity
  • Duration and reversibility
  • Future-resource effects
  • Cleanup difficulty and public burden
  • Scientific confidence
Layer 03 — Price

Federal Base Rate

What each weighted unit costs the public.

  • Cleanup cost
  • Restoration cost
  • Market-transition cost
  • Public infrastructure cost
  • Public-health protection cost
  • Congressional policy rate
  • Established federal assessment
  • Responsible-party recovery cost

Placement of Assessment

Upstream or downstream — where the assessment applies.

The same measured harm can be assessed where materials enter the economy or where harm leaves the facility. Placement changes administration and precision — not the underlying environmental debt.

Upstream Assessment — Point of Entry

Applied at

  • Extraction
  • Import
  • Production
  • Primary manufacture

Administrative advantages

  • Fewer entities to assess
  • Easier administration
  • Broad market influence from a small number of assessment points
Assessment enters the cost structure before the material disperses through the economy
Downstream Assessment — Point of Harm

Applied at

  • Discharge
  • Smokestack
  • Facility
  • Landfill
  • Point of consumption

Administrative advantages

  • Closer to the actual harm
  • Geographically precise
  • Useful for facility-level accountability
Assessment lands where the harm physically occurs — facility by facility

Rate Discipline

Marginal damage meets marginal abatement.

Marginal Damage
The additional harm created by one more unit of pollution — the incremental public burden each further ton, pound, or gallon imposes on health, water, land, and future government cost.
Marginal Abatement Cost
The cost of preventing, reducing, cleaning up, or transitioning away from that same unit — the price of not creating the harm in the first place.

Where the two curves meet defines the economically disciplined zone for a policy rate: below it, harm remains underpriced; above it, abatement capital is misallocated. Real methodologies require category-specific research, public review, legal analysis, and sensitivity testing before any rate is proposed.

A defensible rate is discovered, not declared.

Evidence Confidence Framework

Five levels of evidence. No unlabeled certainty.

Every record in the ledger is graded before it is priced. A figure's confidence level is displayed as prominently as the figure itself.

1Level

Verified direct measurement

Instrumented, continuous, or sampled measurement of the harm itself, independently validated against calibration standards.

Verified
2Level

Verified regulatory record

Official filings, permits, monitoring reports, and enforcement records drawn directly from government sources.

3Level

Strong modeled estimate

Peer-reviewed models with validated inputs, disclosed sensitivity ranges, and reproducible parameters.

Estimated
4Level

Preliminary estimate

Early-stage modeling with material data gaps — suitable for screening and prioritization, not for assessment.

Insufficient evidence

A record exists but cannot support a liability figure — displayed without a dollar value until evidence improves.

Safeguards

Legal and ethical safeguards are part of the methodology.

Defensibility is designed in, not appended. Each safeguard below is a standing operational control — not a policy aspiration.

  • No unsupported accusations

    Records assert only what evidence supports; neutral language is enforced platform-wide.

  • Fact and estimate distinction

    Verified facts and modeled estimates are never displayed in the same register.

  • Transparent methodology

    Every formula, factor, and rate basis is published, citable, and reproducible.

  • Correction rights

    Named entities may submit corrections with supporting evidence at any time.

  • Appeal process

    A structured dispute pathway precedes any final record status.

  • Public rulemaking compatibility

    Methodologies are structured for notice-and-comment adoption by public authorities.

  • Conflict-of-interest disclosures

    Council members disclose interests and recuse where required.

  • Independent audit

    Calculations, records, and allocations are examined by outside auditors.

  • Version-controlled calculations

    Every result carries its methodology version and can be recomputed exactly.

  • Privacy and cybersecurity controls

    Federal-grade data handling governs every record and submission.
